silver / orbiter

orbiter

orbiter is silver's desktop and its IDE in one app. It runs as a window on Linux or as the whole screen on orbiter-os, on a PC or a phone. Every app it starts is a silver module hosted in its own sealed view, drawn into orbiter's panes, debugged in place, and swapped live when its source changes.

01The desktop

With nothing open, orbiter shows the apps screen: every silver module that names a section with export software ['games'] (games, creative, everyday, simulation, developer, communication), plus the featured programs built from source in featured/. A press runs a built program or opens the module. Typing filters the cards, and Enter opens the first match. Behind everything is the chosen scene, a live 3D backdrop (Neptune here).

The system bar runs along the top: orbiter's button on the left, the selected window's menus and title in the middle, and on the right + (add a widget) and the widget column's toggle. The task bar along the bottom holds one button per running app.

Widgets

A module that says export widget ['3x5'] can live on the desktop. + adds an empty widget, which offers every such module; a pick starts that app hosted in the widget's box. On a wide desktop the widgets stack in the right 30%; on a narrow one they sit above the icons as tabs.

02Menus

orbiter never puts a dialog over your work. Menus slide out of the left edge as a drawer over a blur of what is under them, and the app keeps its width. The system menu (orbiter's button) is a column of views: the navigator, scenes, Wi-Fi, mouse, keyboard, display and sound, with power at the foot. Its own page is the agent platform (below).

Each window's own menu opens from its name in the bar. A GTK or Qt program's menu bar is read over D-Bus and shown here too, so the program itself draws no menu bar. In a drawer, typing filters the entries, the arrows move, Enter runs, and left and right step to the next menu. A tap of Alt lights the bar for the keyboard.

03Windows

Every window opens maximized in the split. Split adds a pane beside it; the seam drags. A docked window has no title bar of its own: the system bar is its title.

Floating windows

Drag a window down out of the system bar and it comes free: rounded, shadowed, with its own strip (its menus, title and close). It is moved by the strip and sized from an 8 px ring just outside its edge; every move eases. Let go at the top edge, or double-click the strip, and it docks again.

Linux programs

orbiter is also a Wayland compositor (wlroots, with Xwayland). A program runs from the navigator's search or a pinned card; its window lands in the pane that started it, each window on its own headless output so it can be any size. A second window of the same program opens as its own floating pane, and fullscreen asks get a real screen window while orbiter pauses its own drawing.

04Editor & navigator

A file opens in the editor over a frosted copy of the scene. The bar shows its path as crumbs (src / silver / trinity / chart.ag); a crumb opens that folder's menu.

The navigator

Ctrl+Space (or the system menu's Navigator) opens the navigator in the docked view. Down the left: users, projects and drives. Across the top, the files git sees as changed, with the line counts and make commit. The body lists the folder (modules first), as a list or as cards, and the field at the foot searches the whole file index. File open and save as both go through it.

A module's menu

A module's name in the bar opens its menu: a new instance, or one under the debugger; its launch properties (each [ Launch ] member, read from its export, edited in place); Run, Console, Split, Screenshot, Hide; Build and Clean build. A running app's menu lists its instances first, each with its Code.

05Agents

orbiter runs the agent CLI you already have (claude, codex or gemini) and never bundles one. In any trinity app, Ctrl+Shift+S starts the exchange: drag a region of the screen, press Enter, and the capture goes to your agent with your words. The agent and model you run are two rows of buttons, read from the app's .agi. The avatar is drawn live in 3D: it turns away while the agent works, its core flame lights when an edit is ready, and it carries across a live reload.

The agent's work comes back as statuses over the app's socket (busy, note, diff, done, needs); each edit shows as its own diff, and Ctrl+← asks the agent to rewind its files.

06On a phone

On orbiter-os the phone's init sets SILVER_MOBILE (the Fairphone 6 here, 1116 × 2484 at 2×), and every trinity element takes a :use_mobile style state. There is no task bar; menus run full height; a held press low on the screen goes home; a press and drag scrolls with momentum. No app takes the keyboard's focus on its own: only your tap does.

The keyboard

The keyboard module is an input device trinity knows nothing special about. Japanese is a flick keyboard on kana; English and Chinese pinyin are QWERTY. Words are offered from tables built at export from JMdict, the Tanaka corpus, CC-CEDICT and Tatoeba, and a whole sentence is offered first (a Viterbi pass over words and word pairs). There is a mic key for dictation, swipe typing in English, and an emoji page.

Calls and texts

The phone app talks to ModemManager over D-Bus (and mmsd-tng for picture messages); the signal and carrier sit at the right of the system bar, and a ringing call opens the app. These two shots ran against modem/tests/fake_mm.py, the stand-in ModemManager.

07silver-host: how apps run

orbiter never links the apps it shows. Each is a silver module built as a library and started by its own launcher, which silver-host turns into a sealed process. Pictures, sound and input cross between them through shared GPU textures and ring buffers, so the app draws at full speed and orbiter only composes.

The process tree of the console app hosted in the pane above, taken from that run:

orbiter ─┬─ console          the launcher
         │    └─ console     holder: namespaces, the view's root
         │         └─ console   pid 1 in the view, then the app
         │              └─ bash     the shell the console runs
         └─ orbiter          the shell itself

What the app sees: no other process (two in its /proc), no session bus or SSH agent, no write outside its own home and /tmp. It keeps the network. Every instance of one app shares one view, so a second Steam window meets the first. The host learns the app's real pid from its first message, so orbiter can stop it, attach the debugger to it, measure it and take its textures.

08Debugging & live reload, seen from orbiter

The debugger

A module's menu has New instance and Debug. A debug instance's task button grows a pause; paused, it shows continue, step over, step in and step out, and the pane shows the source where it stopped. The console panel (the button at the bottom right) holds the app's log, its assembly and its call stack.

Seen while making these shots: a paused instance stopped inside library start-up (pthread_key_create) and the pane opened its source at the top rather than at the stopped line; a debugged app also takes a steady stream of stop signals. Both are open in AGENTS.md's lists.

Live reload

When an app's source is newer than its library, the host rebuilds it, loads the new library and builds the new instance on a worker thread while the old one keeps drawing. The switch itself takes about a millisecond on the main thread; the old instance is destroyed and its library closed on a separate thread. Members marked persist are carried from the old image to the new one; orbiter itself carries the exchange (the avatar, both boxes, its blur) so nothing on screen blinks. A failed build keeps the running app.

The console reloaded after its source was touched. Its log said:

[console] reloading (cwd .../share/silver-console)
[console] switch: 0ms
[console] reload complete
[console] old instance closed: destroy 24ms workers 0ms close 49ms
